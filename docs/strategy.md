# Strategy

What really matters to get characters with high skills and a rich family, taken from the game's code (version **1.6.36**). The formulas are in the other documents, linked in each part.

## Summary

1. **Choose a spouse for intelligence.** It is what children inherit most.
2. **Ludus before age 6.** Otherwise the child will never be able to study again.
3. **Always pay for the best school.**
4. **Make your children lawyers (Lawyer).** It is the best-paid trade.
5. **Few children.** When the head dies, the fortune is split among all of them.
6. **Spend the influence before the head dies.** The heir receives only a fifth.
7. **Designate an heir.** If there isn't one, you lose almost all the prestige and all the influence.
8. **Cure the sick and wounded right away** and never have negative income.

## Spouse and children

- Children mostly inherit **intelligence** (the parents' without trait bonuses), and intelligence makes them learn everything faster.
- Look for **Strong** or **Brilliant** spouses. If both parents have the trait, the child inherits it half of the time. Avoid **Weak**, **Simple Minded** and **Deformed**.
- With influence 100 or prestige 2,000, **patrician** candidates appear; with a higher class, candidates have better skills.
- Some candidates bring a bonus for the household ("and their …"), such as family health up to ×1.5.
- To have children without trouble, the spouse should be **under 38**.

More: [General mechanics](general-mechanics.md#stackable-traits), [Jobs](jobs.md#when-looking-for-a-spouse-6d52).

## Studies and work

- Ludus from **age 4 to 6** → Grammaticus 9 to 10 → Rhetor (**deliberative**) 12 to 13 → Philosophy 16 to 17.
- Always the most expensive school possible: they learn more and get higher.
- From age 25 there is almost no learning. No austerity while there are children studying.
- **Lawyer** is the best-paid trade. Personality multiplies the wage: **Sly** ×1.4 and **Ambitious** ×1.25; avoid **Trusting** ×0.4.
- At age 7, "Shaping a personality" comes up: the second option gives the child **your own personality**.
- Two or more relatives in the same trade earn a little more each year. A **Physician** at home protects the family's health.

More: [Education](education.md), [Jobs](jobs.md).

## Money and inheritance

- The family's **stewardship** decides how many properties you can have and, in classes 6 and 7, how much money you can keep. What is over gets stolen.
- Invest the surplus in land or livestock; **not in horses**. Marry off the unmarried: they pay a yearly tax.
- When the head dies, the heir receives:
  - the money and properties **divided among all living children** (also those married elsewhere);
  - the influence **further divided by 5**;
  - the prestige, whole.
- "Move out?" lets a child who is not the heir leave with their share. Done early, it is cheap and they no longer count in the split.
- Without an heir, the game adopts a boy: influence to 0 and prestige ÷10.

More: [Properties](properties.md).

## Health

- Babies and people over 55 die the most. Have **two children until the eldest is over 10** and prepare the heir before the head reaches 55.
- A sick person has **×10** risk of dying; a gravely wounded one, **×50**. Cure them right away.
- With **negative income**, the family dies much more.
- Funerals: the **middle option** (household health ×1.5, 3 months). **Never the small one** (×0.8).
- An **Aesculapian snake** is cheap and improves the household's health ([Pets](pets.md)).
- With an old head, fewer than **30 people at home**: otherwise their risk of dying is multiplied.

## War

- Consul, Praetor and Military Tribune always go to war: keep another heir in reserve.
- When in doubt, the option that doesn't risk your life. Never "Carefully sneak past the guard". More: [Military events](military-events.md#summary).
